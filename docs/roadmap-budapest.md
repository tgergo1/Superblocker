# Budapest roadmap

Working list for the automated superblock tool, targeted at Budapest. Sources: the "Modern City Traffic Design Ideas" and "Open map and traffic data sources" research docs (2026-10-15) and `docs/research/complete-automated-superblock-calculator-gap-analysis.md`.

Rules: free tools and open data only; every feature ships with tests; screening outputs stay outside the implementation gate.

## Done

- [x] Last crossings sealed: the 7 entries still leaking ran through four or five superblocks and needed more than the 8 filters per entry the seal pass allowed. With up to 30, no main-road entry reaches another main-road junction through residential streets any more (0 of about 4,200), for 14 more filters and 4 more destination-only gates (1,308); no junction is cut off and the run time is unchanged. Skipping repeat split attempts on cells that failed to split was also tried for speed: it saved nothing and changed the plan, so it was dropped.
- [x] Cells under the minimum checked: merging a small cell with a neighbour even when the pair is oversized, then splitting the pair again along another street, fixes 10 small cells part-way through sizing but none of the 5 small superblocks left in the final plan (2.7, 3.4, 3.9, 4.1 and 4.8 ha). Every neighbour of those 5 is itself over the maximum (17 to 3,300 ha, hillside and outer-district blocks with no street to split on), so no merge and re-split can bring both pieces into range. The plan barely changed and took 5 s longer, so the change was not kept; the 5 stay as small superblocks.
- [x] Crossing search starts inside the superblock: the seal pass now follows only routes that drive into the superblock from its entry. Before, when the nearest route from an entry ran onto a neighbour's street or a street no cell owns, the pass found no street of its own to filter and gave up on that entry, leaving its real crossings open; it also spent filters on routes that belonged to the neighbour. Entries with a way out at another main-road junction fall from 37 to 7 (of about 4,200 now that fewer streets are closed), with about 400 fewer crossing filters and 54 fewer destination-only gates (1,304). Main roads over the 10% threshold fall from 2,637 to 2,241 and diverted peak traffic from 96,100 to 90,900 vehicles per hour.
- [x] Routes out along a split street sealed: the crossing seal now also closes a route that enters a superblock from one main road and leaves along the open half of a split street to another, filtering the superblock's last street before the split street. Counted per main-road entry, for routes that start into the superblock itself, entries with a way out at another main-road junction fall from 235 to 37 of about 3,400, with 159 more filters. (The earlier per-component crossing count rises as filters split components, so it is no longer used.)
- [x] OpenFreeMap vector basemap: set `VITE_BASEMAP_STYLE_URL` (for example OpenFreeMap's no-key `https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/positron`) to draw the map on vector tiles; if the style fails to load, the map falls back to the raster OpenStreetMap tiles.
- [x] Destination-only gates on the map: the app draws them as amber "C" signs with their own legend entry, hover text and street action ("Behajtani tilos, kivéve célforgalom" with an enforcement camera), apart from planters and bus gates.
- [x] Split-street filters move before they become gates: when the access repair would reopen the filter halfway along a split street, it first tries the street's other sections, nearest the middle first, for one whose closure strands nothing, and only then falls back to a destination-only gate. On Budapest 103 filters move, saving 77 camera gates (1,357 left).
- [x] Split streets keep their filter as a gate: when the access repair had to reopen the filter halfway along a residential street two superblocks were split along, the street became a through route between two main roads, and 92% of the traffic the screening pushed onto minor streets ran along such streets. That filter now becomes a destination-only gate instead, and access checks route as a resident. On Budapest minor streets gaining traffic fall from 840 to 660, roads over the 10% threshold from 2,808 to 2,563, and changes reopened for access from 2,101 to 750; gates rise to 1,434.
- [x] Crossings out at a shared corner: the crossing seal now also closes routes that leave on a main road from a neighbour's street at a junction both superblocks touch. On Budapest crossing routes fall from 54 to 38 (direct ones stay at 6), with 72 more filters. Pruning seals made redundant by later ones was tried and dropped: it reopened more crossings than it saved filters.
- [x] Undividable blocks reported as such: a cell over the maximum where every street split, residential ones included, leaves a piece too small now has the size status "undividable block" instead of "over the maximum", and the summary counts them apart. On Budapest all 43 oversized superblocks are undividable blocks (median 24 ha); none is left simply over the limit.
- [x] No junction left without car access: pockets ringed by several filters, which the access repair could not reopen one change at a time, now get destination-only gates on the changes around and inside them until residents can drive in and out. On Budapest junctions cut off fall from 128 to 0, with 76 more gates (457 in all).
- [x] Destination-only gates on crossings: a crossing between neighbouring superblocks where every filter would cut a street off now gets a "kivéve célforgalom" gate instead of staying open. On Budapest direct crossings fall from 148 to 6 and all crossing routes from 214 to 54, with 381 gates in total and no junction newly cut off.
- [x] Destination-only gates for the last through routes: where every cut that would close a superblock's through route also strands streets, the closing design goes in and those cuts become camera-enforced "kivéve célforgalom" (except destination traffic) gates. Residents, deliveries and visitors still drive in. Access checks now route as a resident. On Budapest all 2,598 superblocks pass validation (was 2,555), with 77 gates and no junction newly cut off.
- [x] Sealed crossings between neighbouring superblocks: where streets of two superblocks meet at a junction on the residential street between them, a car could enter from one main road and leave on another through both, unseen by either superblock's own check. A filter now goes on the first street of the second superblock (or the last of the first) when the street's two ends can still reach each other by car. On Budapest: direct crossings 242 → 148, 2,548 filters added, no junction newly cut off (128 before and after). The trade-off is more diverted traffic: roads over the 10% screening threshold rise from about 1,900 to 2,705.
- [x] Fewer sectors where a cut keeps stranding a street: superblocks that still fail validation after the redesign rounds are designed again with three, then two sectors (fewer cuts, each with more room for local access), with the access repair after each try. On the offline Budapest graph: 2,555 of 2,598 superblocks pass validation instead of 2,484; 128 junctions stranded instead of 142.
- [x] Motorways and trunk roads count as through corridors in road coverage, also where they cross a cell on a bridge or end at the city edge without bounding one. On the offline Budapest graph eligible road coverage rises from 99.44% to 99.83%, which clears the coverage blocker of the topology gate. The gate now waits only on the study-area polygon (supplied when the plan runs on the city boundary) and on the superblocks that fail validation (43 after the sector fallback).
- [x] No one-way traps: a design now has to keep a way back out to an entry from every street that had one, not only a way in; a one-way change that lets cars drive in but never out falls back to the connectivity-preserving territory plan. On the offline Budapest graph: 2,484 of 2,598 superblocks pass validation instead of 2,443, 1,882 access changes reopened by the repair instead of 2,073; 142 junctions stay stranded instead of 116.
- [x] Redesign after the access repair: a street the repair pass reopened is kept open, and each superblock it left with a through route is designed again; the solver treats kept-open streets as very costly to cut and finds another cut where one exists. Up to three rounds of redesign and repair. On the offline Budapest graph: 2,443 of 2,598 superblocks pass validation instead of 2,388 of 2,599, and 116 junctions stay stranded instead of 145.
- [x] Every street stays reachable by car: each superblock was designed and validated on its own, treating every junction on its edge as an entry that traffic can reach. A filter on every section of a street two superblocks were split along made the junctions on it unreachable, and filters inside the superblocks behind them then stranded more streets: the whole plan left 7,430 of the 25,818 junctions reachable today with no car access. Now a split street gets one filter halfway along; a superblock is designed only from entries joined to a main road along open boundary roads; and a final pass routes on the whole planned city and reopens, for each stranded pocket, the one access change that reconnects it (a one-way first, then a split-street filter, then a filter inside a superblock) and validates the superblocks it touches again. On the offline Budapest graph: 145 junctions stranded instead of 7,430, 1,215 access changes reopened, 2,388 of 2,599 superblocks pass validation (all 2,599 passed before only because they were checked one at a time). The plan reports the stranded junctions and reopened changes.
- [x] Traffic screening routes over the whole planned city when a trip has no detour inside its superblock's ring, so displaced traffic is assigned instead of dropped: unrouted cut-through pairs fall from 81% to 1%. The screening is now heavier and more honest: 1,757 boundary roads over the increase threshold instead of 221, 758 minor streets loaded (mostly the open halves of split streets), 72,600 added boundary vehicle-km in the central scenario instead of 8,500.
- [x] Traffic simulator, with and without the plan: `POST /api/v1/simulation/capacity` (and `/capacity/stream`) builds gravity demand between grid zones, calibrates today's peak so the average trip takes 1.3x its empty-street time, and assigns it to user equilibrium (Frank-Wolfe, BPR) on today's network and on the planned one. Demand is then raised until each network jams (trips take 2x as long as on empty streets), which gives each network's capacity. The UI panel "Can the city still move?" shows the capacity curve, trip times and a map of where traffic grows or falls. On a cached whole-Budapest OSM graph (34,641 junctions, 3,651 cells) a run takes about 110 s: 426,000 peak-hour car trips, average trip 11.0 min today and 13.4 min with the plan (+23%), capacity 1.61x today's peak without the plan and 1.17x with it (-27%), with fixed demand (no traffic evaporation). It also finds 11,471 junctions with no car access left once every filtered section is closed, which points at modelling filters as points.
- [x] Impact model v2, one model end to end: the traffic simulator routes movement by movement (OSM turn restrictions, junction delays by control, U-turns only at dead ends), keeps destination-only camera gates open to residents, generates trips from residents (Meta HRSL) and jobs (Overture places and buildings) with 15% through main-road gateways, and lets trips respond to the plan (elasticity -0.5). `app/services/impact/environment.py` turns volumes and speeds into NO2 fitted to the ten Budapest stations' 2025 means (OpenAQ) and CRTN peak-hour noise, counted at residents' homes. `scripts/fetch_open_data.py` fetches the layers; GTFS shapes give bus routes and Overture gives schools and fire stations when Overpass is unreachable. Motorways and trunk roads are never modified. Whole-Budapest run (after the review fixes in #83): 2,313 superblocks, 646 bus gates, 0 motorway changes; 238,000 peak car trips; trip time +21% with today's trips and +21% once 8.7% of trips are not made by car; car-km +10% and +4%; capacity -19%; 11 junctions cut off instead of 3,524. NO2 fit R² 0.59 (5.0 µg/m³ leave-one-out error); population-weighted NO2 22.4 → 22.8 µg/m³; 169,000 residents get at least 1 µg/m³ less and 307,000 at least 1 more; 281,000 homes 3 dB quieter and 240,000 louder. Still missing: open traffic counts for calibration.
- [x] Open land is not an oversized superblock: a cell over the maximum where most land lies more than 100 m from any street in or around it (forest, rail land, an airfield with a road through it) is reported as mostly open land, with the land its streets reach, instead of as oversized. Splitting it would only divide fields. On the offline Budapest graph, of the 163 superblocks over 20 ha, 120 are mostly open land (median 48 ha) and 43 stay oversized (median 24 ha).
- [x] Housing estates split along living streets: estates (lakótelepek) are often served only by living streets, which were never split candidates, so an estate stayed one cell of 50-100 ha. A cell that no main road or residential street divides may now split along a living street as a last resort, filtered on every section like a residential split street. On the offline Budapest graph: superblocks with through routes over 40 ha fall from 93 to 74 and over 20 ha from 212 to 163; 2,199 superblocks are within the size range instead of 2,066. All 2,599 cells pass validation; boundary roads over the increase threshold 221 instead of 222; calmed street length 2,816 km instead of 2,859 km, since split living streets now count as boundary streets.
- [x] Cell kinds: every cell reports whether it has through routes. A cell whose streets inside are all dead ends off one entry is already calm and needs no filters, so its size no longer counts against the plan; a cell with no streets inside is counted as non-street land and left out of the superblock list. Each cell also reports whether it is within, under or over the size range, and the plan counts oversized and undersized superblocks proper. On the offline Budapest graph: 2,285 superblocks with through routes, 222 already-calm cells and 480 street-free cells; 212 superblocks over 20 ha and 7 under 6 ha.
- [x] Default policy: keeping busy residential streets inside superblocks is now the default (API, partitioner and UI). The older behaviour stays available as `minor_road_boundaries: "allow"`. On the offline Budapest graph the default plan goes from 1,206 superblocks (median 42 ha, 1,350 minor roads loaded, worst boundary increase 623%) to 2,205 (median 15.7 ha, 1 minor road, 142%).
- [x] Every superblock has an entry: a split whose cut is not a street (a straight extension past the end of one) could leave a piece whose streets reach no boundary road, only the neighbour across the cut. Such a piece has no entries and can never pass validation. That split is now rejected and the next street tried. On the offline Budapest graph every one of the 2,507 superblocks passes validation (16 failed before), which clears the turn-aware validation blocker.
- [x] Compact splits: a split is chosen by how evenly it divides the area and by how compact its least compact piece is (Polsby-Popper, measured on the ground), so cells are no longer cut into long strips when a cut the other way is as even. On the offline Budapest graph: superblocks thinner than about an 8:1 strip fall from 473 to 202 and the median compactness rises from 0.52 to 0.65; 2,387 right-sized superblocks instead of 2,416, 125 over 40 ha instead of 122, 222 boundary roads over the increase threshold instead of 194.
- [x] Split lines reach the boundary: a split line clipped to its cell ended on the boundary only up to rounding, and a line that stops a hair short splits nothing. This alone kept 116 of the cells over 40 ha whole. Clipped lines are now pushed about 10 cm past the boundary. On the offline Budapest graph: 2,538 superblocks (2,416 at or under 40 ha instead of 2,201), 122 cells over 40 ha instead of 221, 194 boundary roads over the increase threshold instead of 218, 56 cells under the minimum instead of 73.
- [x] Uneven splits: when no split of an oversized cell leaves both pieces at the minimum size, one piece may be down to a quarter of the minimum, and the next merge round joins it to a neighbour (never its oversized sibling). On the offline Budapest graph: 2,422 superblocks (2,201 at or under 40 ha instead of 2,143), 221 cells over 40 ha instead of 257, 218 boundary roads over the increase threshold instead of 228; 73 cells stay under the minimum instead of 55, and the worst single boundary-road increase rises from 47% to 279%.
- [x] Splits follow the street: an oversized cell is split along the whole street it is cut on, followed through its junctions and bends to the boundary roads, instead of a straight extension of one segment that cut through the blocks beside it. Every section of the street becomes a boundary road (a quiet residential street gets a modal filter on each section). Cells still over the maximum after a split are split again in the same pass. On the offline Budapest graph: 2,400 superblocks (2,143 at or under 40 ha), 257 cells over 40 ha instead of 336, worst boundary-road load increase 47% instead of 142%, 228 boundary roads over the increase threshold, runs take about 80 s instead of 106 s.
- [x] Minor-boundary repair: with "Keep busy residential streets inside superblocks", any cell over the maximum size splits along a quiet residential street (never a busy rat run), and that street gets a modal filter so diverted traffic stays on main roads. Oversized-cell splits in the default mode get the same filter. On the offline Budapest graph the option now gives 2,205 superblocks instead of 1,004, median 15.7 ha instead of 59 ha, 336 cells over the maximum instead of 701, 1 minor road loaded instead of 25, and 230 boundary roads over the increase threshold instead of 1,207; runs take 106 s instead of 76 s.
- [x] Entry-pair cap: the traffic cut is measured over every entry pair (cap raised from 200 to 20,000; any sample left is spread over all sides). On the offline Budapest graph, removed through traffic rises 22% in circulation-sector mode (24,600 to 30,100 veh/h) and 4% in superblock mode, for under 3% more run time.
- [x] Transit in the pilot ranking: superblocks where under half the streets are within walking distance of a stop rank 15% lower, the "Where to start" list says so, and the map outlines them in purple.
- [x] Import the local planning stack (registry, jobs, planning, evidence, SUMO adapter, optimizer, engineering compiler, approvals, workbench).
- [x] Boundary-road impact estimate with evaporation scenarios, map view and ranked road list.
- [x] Bus and tram aware filters: filters on OSM bus, trolleybus and tram routes become camera bus gates; other filters become emergency bollards.
- [x] Emergency response check from OSM fire and ambulance stations, before and after, with and without exemptions.
- [x] Reclaimed space: calmed street km, carriageway m² and candidate plazas per superblock.
- [x] Option to keep busy residential streets inside superblocks instead of making them boundary roads.
- [x] School streets: timed closures in front of schools and kindergartens inside superblocks; crossing and 30 km/h treatment for schools on main roads.
- [x] Pilot-first phasing and 30 km/h safety: trial and build costs per superblock, interior injury-risk range, and a "Where to start" ranking with map badges.
- [x] Health co-benefit screening: interior noise (dB) and NO2 (%) ranges per superblock, and main roads that get noticeably louder.
- [x] Oversized cells: cells over five times the maximum size with no main road split along residential streets (not in circulation-sector mode), and split lines now work in concave cells. Split streets become boundary roads of both halves. On the offline Budapest graph this turns 782 superblocks into 1,206, shrinks the largest cell from 12,236 ha to 5,276 ha, and raises residents with noticeably quieter streets from about 19,000 to 49,000 (stand-in population grid); two main roads get up to 3.8 dB louder.
- [x] Health per person: with a population grid, noise and NO2 means are weighted by residents, and the plan counts residents whose streets get noticeably quieter (3 dB or more).
- [x] Residents per superblock from GHS-POP (`POPULATION_PATH`): residents and density per superblock, share of residents in superblocks, residents poorly served by transit, and a pilot ranking by residents per hectare.
- [x] Walking access to transit: share of each superblock's streets within 400 m of a bus, tram, metro or train stop, from OSM or a GTFS feed such as BKK's (`GTFS_PATH`); poorly served superblocks are flagged.
- [x] Offline street data: networks load from a local Geofabrik or BBBike extract (`OSM_EXTRACT_PATH`) instead of Overpass.
- [x] Circulation-sector mode (Ghent-style): district-sized sectors bounded by main roads only.
- [x] Telraam importer: hourly citizen counts become peak-hour traffic observations, matched to OSM streets.
- [x] Performance, second pass: whole-city circulation-sector runs finish in about 3 minutes instead of more than 10.
- [x] Performance, first pass: compiled sampled centrality and cached path searches take a Budapest run from about 93 s to 45 s.

## Next

Superblock creation comes first; impact, health and data work support it.

1. **Health, next step.** Calibrate the NO2 share against OKIR stations. Resident weighting is done. The gap between people-weighted and per-superblock results came from outer districts left as single cells of 1,000-12,000 ha (no main road to split on) holding about 30% of residents; those now split along residential streets. Remaining: about 5,300 ha of motorway-only or hillside cells, and the 20-100 ha suburban cells that keep their residential streets inside.
2. **Crossings and diverted traffic.** No main-road entry reaches another main-road junction through residential streets after the seal pass (up to 30 seals per entry, only on streets a superblock owns). About 690 minor streets still gain traffic in the screening; check which are split streets with a gate (residents only) and which are real detours. About 930 of the 1,308 destination-only gates come from split streets the access repair had to open; 760 of those are a single block-long section with no other place for a filter. Trying interior filters first instead saved only 68 gates and removed 5% less through traffic, so the order stays.
3. **Performance.** A whole-Budapest plan takes about 97 s. Measured per stage: sizing cells 49 s, constraint enforcement 14 s, the access repair and redesigns 18 s, building the result (traffic screening) 11 s, the gate and crossing passes 6 s. Caching split trials within a cell gave no gain (the second, relaxed pass rarely runs). Next: parallel traffic screening per superblock, and cheaper graph copies in the access passes.
